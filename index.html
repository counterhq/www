( 01 / 04 ) Why

Programmatic should be open. Too much of programmatic advertising happens inside systems that buyers and publishers cannot inspect, change or own.

Counter is built around three simple ideas.

[01]

Own the intelligence

Use open models, fine-tune them on your own data or bring your own.

Agencies, advertisers and publishers can all contribute models and signals.

Your models remain yours.

[02]

Open infrastructure

The bidder is open source. The models are open. The APIs and documentation are open.

Counter runs on ARTF, an open IAB Tech Lab standard designed to let containerised logic run directly inside the programmatic bidstream.

Inspect it. Extend it. Run it yourself or use our managed infrastructure.

[03]

Connect buyers and publishers

Create clearer, more direct paths between demand and publisher supply.

Fewer unnecessary hops. Less reselling. Clearer economics and provenance.

Publishers keep control of their supply and intelligence. Buyers keep control of how it is valued.

01What are you building?

An open execution platform for programmatic media.

Buyers bring their own models and agents. Publishers bring their supply, signals and rules. Counter connects both sides directly and gives them an open place to execute.

02What is ARTF?

ARTF — the Agentic Real Time Framework — is an open standard from IAB Tech Lab for running software directly inside the programmatic bidstream.

A container is a sealed package of software: the code plus everything it needs to run. Because the package is standard, any platform that supports ARTF can run it the same way, without a custom integration.

Instead of sending every auction to another external system, models and other decisioning logic run in containers close to where the auction happens.

This means fewer network hops, lower latency and a standard way to deploy the same logic across supporting platforms.

Counter is built on ARTF rather than a proprietary execution format.

Read the ARTF standard from IAB Tech Lab and the specification on GitHub.

03Is this a DSP or an SSP?

It overlaps with both, but we are not trying to recreate either in the traditional sense.

We are building an open layer for media decisioning and execution.

04What is open source?

The core auction and bidding infrastructure is open source.

We also release an open family of media decisioning models, along with the tools and examples needed to run them.

Your logs are yours. Auction logs, model decisions, campaign activity and agent actions are available to you, with no charge to access or export them.

05Do I need to build my own models?

No.

You can start with open models provided by the community and fine-tune them using your own data.

The platform includes infrastructure for training, evaluating, fine-tuning, testing and deploying models into live buying.

More advanced teams can bring completely custom models.

06How do agents work?

Agents manage the longer-running work around a campaign.

They can monitor performance, adjust settings, run experiments, retrain models, investigate problems and produce reports.

You can use our agent or connect your own. The platform is API-first, so agents are native operators rather than an interface added afterwards.

You can manage a campaign from Claude, ChatGPT or another compatible agent, including recurring tasks that keep running on a schedule.

For example:

“Check this campaign every morning. If CPA rises above £20, investigate why and tell me before making a major change.”
07What does direct supply mean?

Every impression should have a clear path back to the publisher.

Supply can still come through infrastructure partners, but we want to avoid unnecessary reselling and opaque supply chains.

08How can agencies make money?

Transparency does not mean agencies cannot make margin.

Platform fees, optimisation fees, model fees and performance-based economics can all be explicitly built into campaigns.
