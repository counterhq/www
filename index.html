01What are you building?

A new way to buy programmatic media where the buyer has much more control.

Instead of handing the decision to a traditional DSP, agencies and brands can decide how each impression should be valued using their own data, rules and models.

02Is this a DSP or an SSP?

It overlaps with both, but we are not trying to recreate either in the traditional sense.

We are building the layer that connects your buying decisions to publisher supply.

Over time, more of that infrastructure will be open source.

03What does “direct supply” mean?

We want every impression to have a clear path back to the publisher.

That does not mean every publisher needs to integrate with us directly. Supply can still come through partners such as SSPs, as long as the path is transparent and does not pass through unnecessary resellers.

04Do I need to build my own models?

No.

We plan to provide open models and training examples for common problems such as conversion, attention, inventory quality and bid pricing.

Teams with their own data or modelling capability will also be able to build and deploy their own.

05Where do AI agents fit?

Agents can manage the longer-running work around a campaign: monitoring performance, testing ideas, adjusting budgets, retraining models and reporting results.

The real-time buying decision stays fast and predictable.

06How can agencies make money?

Transparency does not mean agencies cannot make margin.

We want commercial terms to be explicit: platform fees, optimisation fees, model fees, performance fees or other agreed economics can all be part of the campaign.

The client should be able to understand what they are paying for.

07Is it open source?

That is the plan.

We want the core buying and execution infrastructure to be open source, so teams can inspect it, run it themselves and own the technology they build on top.

08What does container-native mean?

The buying logic is packaged so it can run close to the supply rather than sending every auction back through a traditional DSP.

For most users, this should simply mean faster decisions, lower infrastructure costs and more control.
