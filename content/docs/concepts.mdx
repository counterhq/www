## Opportunity

An opportunity is one impression offered for sale: a bid request from publisher supply.
It describes the site, the placement size, the floor price and the time allowed to
answer. Counter follows the [OpenRTB](https://iabtechlab.com/standards/openrtb/) and
[ARTF](https://iabtechlab.com/standards/artf/) standards from IAB Tech Lab.

## Strategy

A strategy is your buying logic. Given an opportunity, it decides whether to bid, at what
price and with which creative. Strategies come in two forms:

- **Declarative strategies** are JSON: a base price, a cap, and multipliers by domain and
  placement size. They're quick to write, easy to review and safe for an agent to change.
  The [quickstart](/docs/quickstart) uses one.
- **Code strategies** are your own bidder written in Rust against the Counter SDK. Use
  one when you need logic a declarative strategy can't express, such as calling your own
  model.

## Model

A model scores an opportunity: how likely it is to convert, how much attention it is
likely to get, how suitable the environment is, or what it's worth. Strategies use
models to make better decisions. A single bid can draw on several models at once.

Start with Counter's open models for conversion, attention, quality, context, bid
pricing and incrementality. You can fine-tune them on your own campaign and outcome
data, or bring your own. The models you train are yours.

## Bidder

A bidder is a strategy deployed into the auction. It has an ID, an owner and one or more
**replicas**. Replicas add capacity and resilience, but a bidder always makes one bid
per opportunity, however many replicas it has.

A replica only receives traffic after it has warmed up and passed health checks. When a
replica stops, Counter drains it first so that auctions already in progress can finish.

## Version

Every change to a bidder creates a new, immutable **version**. A version records exactly
what is running: the strategy, or the digest of the built code. Rolling out a version
happens under live traffic: warm, check, switch, drain. Rolling back redeploys an earlier
version as a new one, so the history is never rewritten.

## Auction

For each opportunity, Counter asks the selected bidders for a decision, collects the
answers that arrive before the deadline, and picks the winner. A bidder that is late,
fails or returns an invalid answer is treated as a no-bid for that auction. It never
holds up the auction or affects anyone else's bid.

## Agent

An agent manages your bidders over time. It can read metrics and recent auctions,
investigate changes, test strategies, deploy and roll back versions, and ask for your
approval. Use Counter's agent or connect your own through MCP or the API. An agent can
only act on bidders it owns and within the permissions you give it.

## Outcome

Outcomes are what happens after a bid: wins, clicks, sales, store visits or qualified
leads. Outcomes flow back to your agents and become training data for your models, so
your strategies improve over time.

## Logs and metrics

Every decision is traceable from the impression to the outcome:

**Opportunity → Models → Scores → Decision → Bid → Result → Outcome**

Metrics include auctions per second, bid rate, win share, mean winning CPM and latency.
You see full detail for your own bidders and aggregates for everyone else. Accessing or
exporting your own logs costs nothing extra.
